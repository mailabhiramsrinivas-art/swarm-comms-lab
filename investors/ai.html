The AI Part 2 · Trained and tested on its own

Teaching the swarm to take a formation apart

The second part of the system decides how many drones to send against an enemy formation, and where to put them. We trained it on a 3D game that captures the heart of the problem.

Build a formation and watch it respond → Watch games in 3D

The game in plain words

An enemy formation

Between 4 and 20 enemy drones hold a shape in 3D space. They flee from our drones while trying to stay together.

Only the outside can be hit

Enemies on the outer surface are exposed. Enemies deep inside are shielded by the ones around them, so the formation has to be peeled from the outside in.

Five rounds, fewest drones

Our team has five rounds to remove every enemy. Using fewer drones scores better, so sending too many is a cost, not a safe choice.

How the AI does it

Step 1

Read the shape

It works out which enemies are exposed on the outside and which are shielded inside. Getting this right decides who can be hit right now.

Step 2

Choose the team and place it

It estimates its chance of winning with 1, 2, 3 or more drones and picks the smallest team with at least a 90% chance. Then it decides where each drone starts.

Step 3

Peel from the outside in

Each round it moves its drones and decides who hits whom, so no two drones waste a hit on the same enemy. As the outer layer falls, the inner enemies become exposed.

A real example from the test set: 14 enemies held a formation with 4 shielded inside. The AI chose 6 drones and cleared the whole formation in 3 rounds.

How it learned

  1. Copy an expertA slow planning program searched thousands of possible futures to find good moves. The AI learned by imitating it.
  2. PractiseIt then improved by trial and error, over 20,400 practice games.
  3. Play on its ownIn use, the planner is gone. Only the trained AI makes the decisions.

What this shows

  • A trained AI can read a formation's shape and size its response to it, instead of following fixed rules.
  • It coordinates its own drones so hits aren't wasted on the same target.
  • It is small enough to run in an ordinary web browser, with no server.

What it doesn't show yet

  • Not connected yet. The AI and the communication system have been built and tested separately.
  • A simplified game. Both sides see everything, and a hit is a simple rule.
  • Behind its teacher. It wins 70% of games where the slow planner wins all of them.
  • Too few drones, sometimes. For the largest formations it chose fewer drones than needed a third of the time.
